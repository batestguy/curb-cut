# User research: "What would you want to look up?"

Short conversations with people from Curb Cut's first audience, held before P4 is designed (`PLAN.md` → P3). Their answers decide what the P4 state pages show. The author runs the calls; this page is the kit and the record.

## Who (3–5 people)

| # | Kind of person | Why them |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Someone at JONAPWD or another organisation of persons with disabilities (state chapter is fine) | They cite disability figures in advocacy and budget requests |
| 2 | A journalist who has covered disability, health or poverty | They need a number and a source they can quote quickly |
| 3 | A researcher or lecturer (public health, social work, statistics) | They need definitions, intervals and the data behind a figure |
| 4 | A disabled person who doesn't work in the sector | The plainest test of whether the site answers real questions |
| 5 (optional) | Someone in a state ministry, NCPWD or a state disability board | Policy use: planning, quotas, budgets |

Try to include at least one person outside Bauchi and at least two different disabilities. One deaf or hard-of-hearing participant should be offered a written or WhatsApp-text version.

## Invitation (WhatsApp or SMS)

> Hello [name], I'm building Curb Cut, a free website with measured figures on disability in Nigeria, state by state. Could I have 15 minutes of your time this week for one question: what would you want to look up on a site like that? By phone, WhatsApp call, or in writing if you prefer. I won't use your name unless you say I can.

## The call (15 minutes)

1. **Consent (read out):** "I'll take notes, not a recording. I'll write down your role, not your name, unless you say I can use it. You can stop at any time." Record the answer.
2. **The one question:** "If there were a website with disability figures for every Nigerian state, what would you want to look up?" Let them talk; don't suggest answers.
3. **Follow-ups, only if time allows:**
   - "What would you do with that number? Who would you show it to?"
   - "Where do you get figures like that today? What's wrong with them?"
   - "How do you usually use websites: phone or computer, and with any assistive technology?"
4. **Close:** "Can I contact you again when the state pages are ready, to see if they work for you?"

Don't show the live site before question 2, because it would anchor their answers. Showing it afterwards is fine.
